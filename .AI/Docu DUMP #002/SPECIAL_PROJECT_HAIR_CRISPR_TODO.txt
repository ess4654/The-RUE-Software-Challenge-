PHASE 2: DELIVERY MECHANISM RESEARCH
==========================================================

[ ] 2.1 — Topical CRISPR delivery via lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)
    - LNP formulations that penetrate follicular ostia (the follicle opening)
    - Studies: LNP-mRNA delivery to skin (Moderna/Pfizer COVID tech used LNPs — same platform)
    - Advantage: surface application, no needles
    - Question: can LNPs reach follicle STEM CELLS (bulge region), not just the shaft?

[ ] 2.2 — Intradermal microinjection
    - Direct injection into dermal papilla of target follicles
    - Feasibility: dermatologists already do this (fillers, steroids for alopecia)
    - Question: injection depth required to hit bulge stem cells (~1.5-2mm deep)
    - Question: how many injection sites per body region?

[ ] 2.3 — Electroporation-assisted delivery
    - Use electrical pulses to open cell membranes for CRISPR uptake after topical/injected delivery
    - Devices: existing electroporation tools used in DNA vaccine delivery
    - Question: skin tissue tolerance — pain, scarring risk

[ ] 2.4 — Viral vectors (AAV) — LIKELY UNSUITABLE but document why
    - AAV = gold standard for in-vivo gene therapy
    - Problem: permanent integration, off-target risk, immune response
    - Problem: AAV spreads beyond application site — violates guardrail #1 (head hair)
    - Document: why viral vectors are ETHICALLY and PRACTICALLY wrong for SPH

[ ] 2.5 — Ex vivo approach: extract follicle stem cells, edit, re-implant
    - Remove body follicle stem cells via biopsy
    - CRISPR edit in lab with full off-target screening
    - Re-implant edited cells
    - Advantage: zero off-target risk in vivo
    - Disadvantage: extremely labor-intensive, not scalable
    - Assess: could this work for SMALL areas (face, neck) as proof of concept?

[ ] 2.6 — Ribonucleoprotein (RNP) delivery — CRISPR as protein+guide, no DNA
    - CRISPR Cas9 protein + guide RNA delivered as pre-formed complex
    - Advantage: NO foreign DNA integrates, editing is transient, degrades after action
    - Advantage: faster editing, lower off-target than plasmid/viral
    - This is the LEADING candidate for somatic cosmetic gene editing
    - Deep dive required on RNP + LNP or RNP + cell-penetrating peptide delivery

PHASE 3: SPECIFICITY AND SAFETY
==========================================================

[ ] 3.1 — Guide RNA design for body-follicle-specific AR co-activator
    - Use computational tools: CRISPOR, Benchling, CHOPCHOP
    - Criteria: >90% on-target score, <5% off-target score
    - Must verify: no homology anywhere in genome for scalp hair genes
    - Must verify: no homology in ciliary/inner ear genes

[ ] 3.2 — Off-target analysis for ALL four guardrails
    - Cross-reference guide RNA against: scalp follicle transcriptome, inner ear stereocilia genes, respiratory cilia genes, cardiovascular development genes
    - Tool: GUIDE-seq, CIRCLE-seq, Digenome-seq — existing wet-lab protocols

[ ] 3.3 — Follicle specificity — how to prevent spread to non-target tissue
    - Skin is layered: epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous
    - Follicle stem cells are in the BULGE — a specific niche
    - Question: does the delivery vehicle reach the bulge AND NOTHING ELSE?
    - Research: follicle-targeting ligands, peptides that bind follicle-specific surface markers

[ ] 3.4 — Immune response to CRISPR components
    - Cas9 protein is bacterial — pre-existing immunity in many humans
    - Question: localized delivery vs systemic — is immune response contained?
    - Research: immunogenicity of Cas9 RNP in skin specifically

PHASE 4: PROOF OF CONCEPT PATHWAY
==========================================================

[ ] 4.1 — Source human hair follicle organoids or ex-vivo skin culture
    - Available from: hair transplant clinics (discarded follicles)
    - Alternative: 3D-printed skin models with follicles (research labs)

[ ] 4.2 — In-vitro CRISPR knockout of target gene in body follicle organoids
    - Deliver RNP to organoid culture
    - Measure: follicle shutdown (no new shaft growth in culture)
    - Measure: cell viability (no death in surrounding tissue)
    - Measure: scalp follicle organoids UNAFFECTED (parallel control)

[ ] 4.3 — Animal model assessment
    - Question: which animal has analogous body vs. scalp follicle differentiation?
    - Likely candidate: mice with androgen-dependent hair growth (not all mouse strains)
    - ETHICAL REVIEW REQUIRED before any animal work

[ ] 4.4 — Regulatory pathway mapping

    - Precedent: CRISPR therapy for sickle cell (Casgevy) approved Dec 2023
    - Difference: sickle cell = life-threatening, body hair = quality of life
    - Research: has FDA ever approved somatic gene editing for cosmetic indication?
    - If not: SPH may need to be framed as a medical correction (folliculitis, pseudofolliculitis, hidradenitis suppurativa — all are body-hair-caused DISEASES)
