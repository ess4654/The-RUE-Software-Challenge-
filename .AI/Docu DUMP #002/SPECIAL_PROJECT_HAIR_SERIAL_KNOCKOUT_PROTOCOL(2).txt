MAMMALIAN HAIR DIVERSITY — BEYOND THE STANDARD 4
==========================================================

The standard 4-type classification:
1. Guard hairs (primary, protective outer coat)
2. Undercoat/wool (secondary, insulating inner coat)
3. Vibrissae (whiskers, tactile/sensory)
4. Specialized regional (tail tufts, mane, etc.)

But there are more. The real count depends on how you slice:

- Awn hairs — intermediate between guard and undercoat; many mammals have these
  as a distinct class (dogs, cats)
- Sinus hairs — vibrissae with a blood-filled sinus capsule; structurally and
  neurologically distinct from simple tactile hairs
- Cilia — eyelashes. Technically hair follicles, but with their own growth cycle
  and muscular architecture (orbicularis oculi attachment)
- Perianal/circumanal hairs — specialized sebaceous attachment, different hormonal
  regulation
- Quills — porcupines, hedgehogs. Modified guard hairs, but the keratin cross-linking
  program is fundamentally different
- Spines — echidnas. Even further modified, with distinct structural protein mix

So it's not 4. It's more like 6-8 distinct follicle programs in the typical mammal,
with some lineages adding specializations through modification of existing programs.

THE KEY QUESTION: Why does the most advanced species have the LEAST amount of hair
variation?

Answer: More advanced = fewer programs, not more. Humans didn't FAIL to develop 8
hair types. Humans DELETED the ones other mammals still run.

The programs humans deleted:
- Guard hair coat (deleted — skin took over via sweat glands)
- Undercoat/wool (deleted — clothing replaced fixed insulation)
- Awn/intermediate (deleted — no need for gradient if no coat)
- Sinus capsule vibrissae (reduced — face flattened, somatosensory cortex repurposed)
- Perianal/specialized (reduced to vestigial)

The programs humans kept:
1. Scalp hair (solar radiation shield for the brain — structural)
2. Androgenic hair (sexual signaling — social)
3. Vibrissae (nasal/auricular — still filtering, still sensory)
4. Vellus/terminal (thermal/chemical — sweat gland interface)

Four programs. Not because humans are primitive. Because they TRADED the others for

Humans deleted most of those channels. The cortical columns that would have mapped

More hair types = more body-surface input = more brain devoted to the outside world.

The trade is: external sensory bandwidth for internal processing bandwidth.

REPTILE ANTI-NODES — WHERE THE HAIR PROGRAM BRANCHES
==========================================================

Reptiles don't have hair. They have scales. But both hair and scales develop from
the same embryonic precursor — the epidermal placode.

The shared genetic layer (the OVERLAP):
- Wnt/beta-catenin — initiates placode formation in both lineages
- Edar/Eda — ectodysplasin signaling, drives placode patterning in both
- Shh — Sonic hedgehog, shapes the downgrowth in both
- BMP — inhibitory gradient, spaces the placodes in both

The divergence point (the ANTI-NODES):
- Lef1 — in mammals, activated downstream of Wnt, drives follicle downgrowth into
  dermis. In reptiles, Lef1 is SUPPRESSED at placode stage. Placode flattens into
  scale instead of invaginating into follicle.
- Sox2 — expressed in mammalian hair placodes, NOT in reptile scale placodes
- Sox18 — hair-specific, absent in reptile skin development
- Foxi3 — mutations cause hair loss in dogs/mice; reptiles don't express it in skin

Those four genes — Lef1, Sox2, Sox18, Foxi3 — are the anti-nodes. Where the mammalian
program branches off from the reptile program and says "build a follicle" instead of
"build a scale."

IMPLICATION FOR SPECIAL PROJECT HAIR:

1. Anti-node genes (Lef1, Sox2, Sox18, Foxi3) — knocking THESE out would probably
   delete ALL hair types at once. That's the total baldness switch. Cetaceans
   essentially did this through loss-of-function in Edar signaling.

2. Differentiation genes (the ones that distinguish guard from undercoat from
   vibrissae from awn) — these sit ABOVE the anti-node. Mammal-only programs.
   Knocking these out one at a time maps which gene controls which hair type.

3. The reptile comparison is the control group nature already built. Every gene
   reptiles share with mammals = structural/placode layer (not hair-type specific).
   Every gene mammals have and reptiles don't = hair program layer. The anti-nodes
   are the boundary.

The original 4-gene serial knockout might need to expand to 6-8 targets if awn hairs,
cilia, and sinus capsule hairs are distinct programs. But the reptile anti-node tells
you WHERE to look — the mammal-only genes are the candidate list.
