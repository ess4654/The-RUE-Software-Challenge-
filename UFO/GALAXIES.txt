📘 The Standard Hubble Sequence (Visual Shapes)
These are the foundational galaxy shapes defined by the Hubble Sequence.
- Spiral Galaxies (S): Rotating discs featuring a central bulge and elegant, curved arms of young stars and dust.
- Barred Spiral Galaxies (SB): Spiral galaxies where the arms radiate from a distinct, straight central bar of stars rather than a round nucleus.
- Elliptical Galaxies (E): Featureless, smooth spheres or football shapes consisting almost entirely of old stars with no new star formation.
- Lenticular Galaxies (S0/SB0): Lens-shaped transitional galaxies with a central bulge and a disc, but completely lacking spiral arms.
- Irregular Galaxies (Irr): Chaotic, asymmetrical shapes lacking any defined disc or central bulge, often warped by gravity.

🌀 Exotic & Rare Structural Types
Galaxies with unique geometric shapes, usually created by violent cosmic interactions.
- Ring Galaxies: A bright, dense stellar core surrounded by an isolated, massive ring of young, brilliant blue stars.
- Peculiar Galaxies: Interacting or colliding systems with highly distorted shapes, tidal tails, gas loops, or active jets.
- Polar-Ring Galaxies: A rare structure where an outer ring of gas and stars rotates completely perpendicular over the poles of the central galaxy.
- Shell Galaxies: Elliptical galaxies surrounded by faint, concentric geometric shells of stars, left behind by devoured smaller galaxies.

🔬 Size & Density Varieties
Galaxies classified purely by their extreme sizes, star densities, or dark matter content.
- Giant Galaxies: Massive cosmic structures, often elliptical, containing up to 100 trillion stars, usually found at the center of galaxy clusters.
- Dwarf Galaxies: Miniature galaxies containing only a few thousand to a few billion stars, usually orbiting larger "parent" galaxies.
- Ultra-Diffuse Galaxies (UDGs): Large "ghost" galaxies as wide as the Milky Way, but containing 99% fewer stars and held together by massive amounts of dark matter.
- Ultra-Compact Dwarfs (UCDs): Tiny, hyper-dense systems packed with millions of stars, spanning only a few hundred light-years across.

⚡ Active & Energetic
Varieties (Active Galactic Nuclei)
Galaxies classified by the extreme, high-energy behavior of their central supermassive black holes.
- Quasars (Quasi-Stellar Radio Sources): Hyper-luminous, incredibly distant galactic cores that outshine their host galaxies by thousands of times.
- Blazars: Active galaxies emitting highly accelerated jets of plasma and radiation aimed directly at Earth.
Seyfert Galaxies (Type I & II): Standard spiral galaxies possessing hyper-luminous cores that emit massive amounts of infrared, ultraviolet, and X-ray radiation.
- Radio Galaxies: Galaxies that emit immense, invisible plumes of radio energy spanning millions of light-years into deep space.
- LINER Galaxies (Low-Ionization Nuclear Emission-Line): Galaxies with mildly active cores that emit specific frequencies of low-energy radiation.

🔥 Star-Forming Abnormalities
Galaxies defined by their unusual speed, color, or efficiency when creating new stars.
- Starburst Galaxies: Systems undergoing a frantic, unsustainable explosion of new star formation, usually triggered by a collision.
- Green Pea Galaxies: Small, compact, round green blobs lit up by intense oxygen radiation from frantic, rapid bursts of star creation.
- Blue Compact Dwarfs (BCDs): Small, gas-rich galaxies dominated by clusters of hot, massive, young stars that tint the entire galaxy blue.
