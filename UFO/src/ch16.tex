\section{Project Paperclip}\label{sec:paperclip}

Operation Paperclip is not a conspiracy theory. It is documented American policy, and it is the
single best illustration in this book of why people distrust official statements --- because on
this one, the paranoid version was closer to the truth than the official version, for decades.

Between 1945 and 1959, the United States brought roughly 1,\!600 German scientists, engineers and
technicians to America, along with their families, sanitising their records to permit entry. The
most prominent was Wernher von Braun, technical director of the V-2 programme at Peenem\"unde, who
surrendered with his team in May 1945 and became, by 1960, director of NASA's Marshall Space Flight
Center and the chief architect of the Saturn V.

\figwrap[r]{0.38}{V-2 assembly in the Mittelwerk tunnels, where more people died building the rockets than were killed by them.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:V-2_assembly_Mittelwerk.jpg}{fig:mittelwerk}
The rest of the record: V-2 production at the Mittelwerk underground facility (see Figure~\ref{fig:mittelwerk}) used slave labour
from the Mittelbau-Dora concentration camp, where more people died building the rockets --- an
estimated 20,\!000 --- than were killed by them. Von Braun visited Mittelwerk and held SS rank.
Arthur Rudolph, production manager at Mittelwerk and later project manager of the Saturn V,
voluntarily left the United States in 1984 and renounced his citizenship after the Justice
Department's Office of Special Investigations opened a case. Hubertus Strughold, who became the
``father of space medicine'' at the Air Force School of Aviation Medicine, had directed an institute
associated with human experiments at Dachau. Walter Schreiber, Kurt Blome and others with medical
war-crime associations were considered or employed.

The rationale was the Soviet Union, which ran its own version --- Operation Osoaviakhim ---
removing thousands of German specialists by force in October 1946. Both superpowers built their
space programmes on the same captured technology and the same people.

So what does Paperclip mean for this book?

It establishes, definitively, three things. First, the American government will conceal morally
catastrophic information for strategic advantage, at scale, for decades, and lie about it in
writing. Second, an institutional capacity for that kind of secrecy existed exactly when and where
the UFO era began. Third --- and this is the part that cuts against the conspiracy reading --- it
\emph{came out}. Historians, journalists, the OSI and eventually declassification produced the full
picture. Linda Hunt's \emph{Secret Agenda} (1991) and Annie Jacobsen's \emph{Operation Paperclip}
(2014) are built from released documents.

The usual ufological deployment of Paperclip is the claim that German engineers arrived with
advanced antigravity or saucer technology --- the Haunebu and Vril claims of Section~\ref{sec:aryan} --- and
that American aerospace performance after 1947 derives from it. The reality is that what they
arrived with was liquid-fuelled rocketry, inertial guidance and systems engineering, all of it
documented, all of it traceable in the Saturn V, and all of it quite enough to explain the change.
The V-2's descendants went to the Moon. That is a large enough claim without adding saucers.

The same move is made with Kecksburg (Section~\ref{sec:kecksburg}), where Paperclip is used as the
bridge that carries Die Glocke across the Atlantic and drops it in a Pennsylvania wood in 1965.
Watch the direction of travel in that argument: a documented programme is used to lend credibility
to an undocumented device, and then the undocumented device is used to explain a badged recovery
nobody can produce a file for. Paperclip earns none of that. It proves the government will conceal
and lie about what it imported. It says nothing about what came down.

\section{The Race to Space}\label{sec:racespace}

The sequence, because the dates carry the argument.

\figwrap[l]{0.38}{Sputnik~1: four whip antennas, a radio beacon, and a political earthquake.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sputnik_asm.jpg}{fig:sputnik}
Sputnik 1, 4 October 1957 --- a 58-centimetre aluminium sphere with a radio beacon (see Figure~\ref{fig:sputnik}), and a political
earthquake. Sputnik 2 carried Laika a month later. Explorer 1, the first American satellite,
reached orbit on 31 January 1958 after the public failure of Vanguard, and NASA was created in
July 1958. Luna 2 struck the Moon in 1959; Luna 3 photographed its far side. Yuri Gagarin orbited
on 12 April 1961. Alan Shepard flew suborbital on 5 May 1961, and Kennedy announced the Moon
commitment on 25 May, twenty days later --- which tells you what the programme was for. John Glenn
orbited in February 1962. Valentina Tereshkova flew in 1963. Alexei Leonov walked in space in 1965.
The Soviets lost Komarov in 1967 and the US lost the Apollo 1 crew the same year. Zond 5 sent
tortoises around the Moon in 1968; Apollo 8 sent humans around it that December; Apollo 11 landed
on 20 July 1969.

The astronaut sighting reports are the part relevant here, and they need sorting.

\textbf{Gordon Cooper} is invariably cited. He did describe, in later years, filming a
saucer-shaped object at Edwards in 1957 while a test pilot --- a claim that predates his spaceflight
and for which no film has been produced. He also repeatedly denied seeing anything anomalous on
his Mercury flight, contrary to persistent claims.

\textbf{James McDivitt}, Gemini 4, 1965, reported a cylindrical object with an arm. He later stated
he believed it was the Titan second stage or another piece of his own launch debris, and was
explicit that he did not consider it anomalous. \hi{The photographs frequently attributed to the
sighting are not the object he saw.}

\textbf{Ed White}, same flight, reported the same debris.

\textbf{Apollo 11's} ``something out there'' was tracked, and Aldrin has stated clearly and often
that it was one of the four SLA panels from the Saturn V third stage, travelling on a similar
trajectory, and that he is frustrated at being quoted otherwise.

\textbf{The Apollo 11 ``we've been warned off the Moon'' transmission} is an invention. It appears
in no NASA transcript, no recording, and no contemporaneous source, and traces to a 1979 book by
Maurice Chatelain.

The genuine pattern is that spaceflight puts human beings in an environment full of ice crystals,
vented water and urine, thermal blanket fragments, paint flecks, launch debris and other
satellites, all in vacuum, all brilliantly sunlit against black, with no atmospheric cues for range
or size. Astronauts see unidentified objects constantly and identify almost all of them, and the
ones they cannot identify they describe as debris because they know what the environment contains.

\begin{funfact}
The most persistent astronaut UFO image --- the ``tether incident'' from STS-75 in 1996, showing
numerous notched discs moving near a broken satellite tether --- was resolved by geometry. The
objects are out-of-focus debris and ice close to the camera; the notch is the camera's aperture
diffraction pattern, visible on defocused point sources, which is why every single object has an
identical notch in an identical orientation. The same signature appears in unrelated footage. It is
not a fleet. It is a lens.
\end{funfact}

\section{The Moon Landing is Not A Myth}\label{sec:moonlandingnotmyth}

I include this section because if you cannot evaluate the Moon hoax claim, you cannot evaluate
anything else in this book, and because the Moon hoax is the purest available example of a claim
that is refuted by physical evidence anyone can check.

The evidence, in descending order of how hard it is to argue with.

\textbf{Retroreflectors.} Apollo 11, 14 and 15 placed corner-cube laser retroreflector arrays on
the lunar surface. Observatories --- McDonald, Ap\-ache Point, C\^ote d'Azur, Matera --- have been
bouncing lasers off them continuously for over fifty years, measuring the Earth--Moon distance to
millimetre precision and detecting the Moon's 3.8-centimetre annual recession. Anybody with access
to a suitable telescope can do it. The Soviet Lunokhod reflectors are also there, and the Apollo
ones are in the places the missions said they were.

\textbf{382 kilograms of lunar sample}, distributed to laboratories worldwide including in the
USSR, showing zero water content, high-temperature mineralogy, cosmic-ray exposure ages, solar wind
implantation, micrometeorite impact glass and zap pits, and isotope ratios matching lunar
meteorites found in Antarctica but no terrestrial rock. Fifty years of independent geochemistry by
thousands of scientists across every political bloc. Nobody has produced a plausible account of
faking it, because the material would have to be manufactured in an environment that did not exist.

\textbf{Independent orbital imaging.} The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has photographed all six
landing sites, showing descent stages, rover tracks, experiment packages and disturbed regolith,
with shadows tracking the known sun angles. Japan's Kaguya, India's Chandrayaan, and China's
Chang'e have also imaged the sites --- and it is worth pausing on the fact that the Chinese space
programme has independently confirmed American landing sites.

\textbf{The Soviets tracked the missions}, had every incentive to expose a fraud, had their own deep
space network, and congratulated the crew.

\textbf{400,\!000 participants} across NASA, contractors and universities, and no deathbed
confession in fifty-five years, alongside terabytes of telemetry, thousands of hours of
transcripts, engineering drawings, and hardware in museums.

On the standard objections: the flag appears to wave because it has a horizontal rod along the top
and the astronauts twisted the pole. Shadows appear non-parallel because of terrain and wide-angle
lenses. \hi{There are no stars because the exposure was set for a sunlit surface.} The Van Allen belts
were transited quickly on a trajectory chosen to skirt the densest region, and the measured dose was
about that of a chest CT. The lack of a blast crater is because the descent engine was throttled to
about 3,\!000 pounds of thrust over a broad area in vacuum, which is nothing like a jet on concrete.

\begin{funfact}
My favourite argument is the one from difficulty. Faking the landings in 1969 would have required
film technology that did not exist --- sustained low-gravity dust behaviour, which is visible in
the footage and which nobody could simulate until CGI --- plus faking the sample geology, the laser
retroreflectors, the telemetry, and the Soviets. Actually going was cheaper. It usually is.
\end{funfact}

\section{Transient Lunar Phenomena}\label{sec:transientlunarphenomena}

Here is the genuine lunar anomaly, and it has a NASA catalogue number.

Transient lunar phenomena are short-lived localised brightenings, obscurations, reddish or bluish
glows, and star-like flashes on the lunar surface, reported by competent observers for centuries.
William Herschel reported them. The Royal Greenwich Observatory recorded them. In 1968 NASA
published Technical Report R-277, \emph{Chronological Catalog of Reported Lunar Events}, compiled
by Barbara Middlehurst and Patrick Moore, listing 579 events from 1540 to 1967 --- prepared
specifically because Apollo needed to know whether the Moon was geologically active.

The events cluster remarkably: Aristarchus accounts for roughly a third, with Plato, Alphonsus,
Gassendi, Grimaldi and the Mare Crisium region making up much of the rest. That clustering is the
strongest argument that something real is happening, since observer bias would not select those
specific craters so consistently.

The best-documented case is 3 November 1958, when the Soviet astronomer Nikolai Kozyrev, using the
Crimean Observatory's 50-inch telescope, obtained spectra of Alphonsus showing emission bands he
attributed to $\mathrm{C_2}$ molecules --- a gas emission spectrum from the lunar surface. The
plates exist. Interpretation has been disputed for sixty-five years.

Apollo carried instruments for this. Apollo 15 and 16 flew the mass spectrometer and alpha-particle
spectrometer that detected radon-222 and polonium-210 emanation, with elevated readings over
Aristarchus and around Mare Crisium's edges --- direct evidence of gas release from the interior.
Lunar Prospector confirmed radon events in 1998--99.

The leading explanations are all geological. Outgassing of radon and other volatiles through
fracture systems, lofting dust that scatters light. Electrostatic dust levitation driven by
solar-wind charging at the terminator. Impact flashes, which are now routinely recorded --- NASA's
lunar impact monitoring programme has logged hundreds, and the LCROSS and LADEE missions studied
the exosphere. Piezoelectric or triboluminescent effects from tidal stress. And the seismometers
left by Apollo detected genuine shallow moonquakes for years.

So: real phenomenon, geological mechanism, still incompletely characterised. Which is exactly the
status of Hessdalen in Section~\ref{sec:paranormalskies}, and exactly what a legitimate open anomaly looks like ---
catalogued, instrumented, correlated with independent measurements, and nobody claiming it is a
base.

\section{Hollow Moon}\label{sec:hollowmoon}

Everybody argues about the wrong sentence. The popular version of this section is a shouting match
over whether the Moon ``rang like a bell,'' as though that were still open. It is not open. It was
measured. Apollo~12 dropped its ascent stage onto the surface on 20 November 1969 at roughly 1.7
km/s, and the seismometer the crew had left behind kept sending signal for something close to an
hour. Apollo~13's spent S-IVB booster hit in April 1970 with about eleven times that energy and the
reverberation ran longer still. Later impacts and natural events gave the same profile: a slow rise
to peak, then a decay so gentle the trace looks like a mistake to anyone raised on terrestrial
records. Nobody in the field disputes the data. The papers are published, the tapes exist, the
numbers have been re-reduced repeatedly since.

\actualq{Did the Moon ring for an hour?}{Why is a body with almost no internal loss the one fact
about the Moon that never gets chased --- and would anyone accept the answer if the arithmetic came
back the wrong way?}

\subsection{What the Damping Number Actually Says}\label{sec:lunardamping}

Strip the mysticism and you are left with one quantity: $Q$, the seismic quality factor. $Q$ is
nothing more than a bookkeeping ratio --- how much vibrational energy a material still holds divided
by how much it loses each time it swings. High $Q$ means a nearly lossless material. Low $Q$ means
the energy is being converted to heat almost as fast as it arrives. Earth's crust runs a $Q$ of a
few hundred at best, and water-saturated sediment can fall below a hundred, which is why a quarry
blast in Ohio is finished in seconds. The Moon returns roughly 3,\!000 to 5,\!000, and some deep
estimates run higher still. That is an order of magnitude and change, and it is the entire substance
of the anomaly.

Now the part that has to be said correctly, because the popular version of this argument is wrong
and the error is expensive. \hi{A long ring does not mean hollow. It means lossless.} Those are
different claims and only one of them is defensible. A church bell rings for a minute because bronze
has almost no internal friction, not because there is air inside it; cast the same bell solid and it
still rings, at a different pitch and for a shorter time, but it rings. Meanwhile a bag of wet sand,
which is emphatically not hollow either, absorbs a hammer blow and returns nothing. The variable
that governs how long a struck body sustains a note is internal loss, not internal emptiness.

\figwrap[r]{0.42}{The Apollo~12 record of the ascent-stage impact --- the trace that will not stop.
The argument was never about whether this happened. It is about what a body has to be made of to
produce it.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seismometer_reading_from_impact_made_by_Lunar_Module_ascent_stage_(S69-59547).jpg}{fig:lunarseismo}
So what does the Moon have that Earth does not? Two things, and a coder will recognise both
immediately as separate failure modes that produce the same symptom in the log. The first is
\emph{intrinsic attenuation} --- real loss, energy deleted from the system and dumped as heat. On
Earth that loss is dominated by water. Pore fluid squeezing between grain boundaries under a passing
wave is a friction machine, and it is extraordinarily efficient at eating seismic energy. The Moon
has no pore water, no free volatiles, and no warm wet crust to speak of, so that entire loss channel
is simply absent. The second is \emph{scattering} --- not loss at all, but reordering. The upper few
kilometres of the Moon are megaregolith: rock shattered, re-shattered and stirred by four billion
years of bombardment with no weather and no erosion to heal it. A wavefront entering that layer is
split, reflected and delayed along millions of paths, so instead of one clean arrival you get the
same packet of energy dribbling back to the sensor out of order for the next hour. Nothing was
destroyed and nothing was resonating in a cavity. The signal was scattered into a queue and it drains
slowly.

That mechanism is real, it is well posed, and I am not going to pretend otherwise in order to keep a
better story. What it is not is finished. A model of the lunar coda has free parameters --- scattering
layer thickness, velocity gradient, and above all the split between intrinsic loss and scattering ---
and those parameters get tuned after the fact until the synthetic seismogram resembles the one on the
tape. It reproduces codas. It has never independently predicted one. And the partition between the
two loss channels is still being argued in the literature fifty-five years later, which is a strange
thing to call settled.

There is also a clean test that settles the cartoon version, and I want it on the record because a
hypothesis that cannot be killed is not worth defending. A hollow shell is a resonator with a
geometry, and a resonator with a geometry rings at specific frequencies --- discrete, sharp, and
predictable from the shell's dimensions, the way an organ pipe or a struck wineglass does. That is a
falsifiable prediction, and it fails. The lunar records show a broadband diffuse tail with no
shell-mode line spectrum anywhere in it, which is the signature of scattering in a fractured solid
and is not the signature of a cavity. The rest of the case points the same way: GRAIL mapped the
gravity field to kilometre resolution in 2012 and found a differentiated rocky body --- crust of
roughly 34 to 43 km, mantle, a partially molten layer, a small core near 240 to 350 km. Apollo's
network caught shear waves crossing the interior, and shear waves do not propagate through a vacuum.
Laser ranging pins the moment of inertia, and with it the mass distribution, tighter than any
conspiracy has room to hide inside.

\hi{So the thin empty shell is dead, and I am the one telling you it is dead.} A book that only
demolishes other people's evidence and never its own is propaganda with footnotes. What survives is
smaller and more durable: the Moon is a solid body with an anomalously lossless interior, the
explanation for that losslessness is a fitted model rather than a predictive one, and the number
everybody has spent fifty-five years arguing about --- how much of the decay is real loss and how
much is reordering --- is still open. That is the crack. It is a narrow one. It is also a real one,
and narrow real cracks are the only kind worth standing in.

\subsection{The Reason Nobody Will Touch the Math}\label{sec:whynobodytouches}

Here is my claim, and I want it stated plainly so it can be attacked. It is not that lunar
seismology is a shunned field --- it is not, the Apollo passive seismic data has been re-reduced and
re-published continuously for decades, and the people doing that work are doing it honestly. What is
shunned is one specific interpretation, and I will grant the obvious first objection: the simplest
reason to avoid the artificial reading is that the evidence for it is thin. I have just spent two
pages saying so myself.

But thin evidence explains why a claim is not \emph{accepted}. It does not explain why the underlying
number is not \emph{pursued}. And that is the gap I am pointing at, because \emph{artificial} is not a
statement about geology. It is a statement about intent.

A natural Moon is an accident --- a splash of debris from the Theia impact that happened to settle
into orbit and happened to end up with one face turned permanently toward us. Accidents need no
author, and an accident can be published without disturbing anything.

An engineered Moon is a different sentence entirely, and before I make that case I have to hand back
two of the arguments usually made for it, because they do not survive contact and using them would
make me exactly the kind of writer this book exists to correct. The tidal lock is not a smoking gun:
nearly every large satellite orbiting close to its primary ends up locked, because tidal friction
drives it there on its own. It is the default outcome, not a decision. Nor is the eclipse coincidence
one --- the Moon subtending almost exactly the Sun's angular diameter is real and it is beautiful,
but the Moon is receding about 3.8 cm a year, so total eclipses are a passing phase we happen to be
alive during. If anything that argues \emph{against} design, because no engineer builds the
showpiece feature with an expiry date.

What does not go away is the consequence. Whatever put that body there, its mass is what stabilised
Earth's axial tilt, and a stable tilt is what gave this planet stable seasons, and stable seasons are
the window in which complex life had time to happen. Remove the Moon and you get a world that wobbles
chaotically and never holds a climate long enough to build anything that thinks. So if the structural
reading is ever vindicated --- if that interior turns out to be built rather than accreted --- then
the habitability of this planet was not luck. It was a specification. It means the Earth is not a
rock that happened to grow biology; it means the Earth is an experiment with an observation deck, and
that something has been on station above it, on the same face, for as long as there has been anything
here worth observing. Twenty-four hours a day. No shift change. \hi{That is what the word artificial
costs, and that is the bill nobody wants to be handed at a funding review.}

\begin{funfact}
Mikhail Vasin and Alexander Shcherbakov published the ``Spaceship Moon'' hypothesis in 1970 in
\emph{Sputnik}, a Soviet popular-science magazine, months after the Apollo~12 coda was recorded.
Their mechanism was wrong and their evidence was thin. But note the timing --- two Soviet writers
looked at an American seismogram and immediately understood that the interesting number was the
decay rate, not the amplitude. That was more than half a century ago, and the decay rate is still
the interesting number.
\end{funfact}

\subsection{Did They Ever Actually Leave?}\label{sec:didtheyleave}

So I will put the question to you the way it deserves to be put, and you can sit with it, because I
am not going to pretend I can close it.

Every culture that left us writing put its gods in the sky and then told us they departed. They
ascended, they withdrew, they went behind the veil, they promised to return. We read those texts as
farewell notes. But a farewell is what it looks like from the ground when something stops introducing
itself. It is not the same as absence. There is a platform in permanent orbit above you with one face
fixed upon this planet --- that part is not in dispute, whatever built it and however it got there ---
and if anything ever was watching from it, it never needed to leave. It only needed to stop talking.

I cannot prove that and I am not going to pretend the seismograms do it for me. What I can say is
that the strange thing here is not that the Moon rang for an hour. The strange thing is how quickly
we agreed it was nothing, and how little machinery exists anywhere to ask the question in a room that
has a budget.

\section{You Have Got To Be Joking!}\label{sec:youhavegotbejoking}

\hi{What is the correct price of a thing that can never be made again?} That is the only question in
this section, and it is not rhetorical. It has an answer, the answer was written down by an
institution that had to choose, and the answer was nine dollars.

(There is no argument to be had about whether it happened: NASA says it did, the 2006--2009 search
under Dick Nafzger and Stan Lebar concluded in its report of 3~November 2009 that the 1-inch
telemetry tapes carrying the raw slow-scan signal from the first human steps on another world were
erased and reused in the early 1980s for want of blank stock, and over seven hundred further boxes of
Apollo-era telemetry have never been found. Nothing to debunk. Move on to the interesting part.)

An erasure is a purchase. Somebody weighed two things against each other and chose. On one side of
In the currency of the day a data-grade reel ran to a
few dollars --- call it nine dollars in today's money for a cassette-sized equivalent --- and even at
institutional prices for 14-inch instrumentation reels the saving on a single tape would not cover
lunch. On the other side of the scale: the master recording of the single most watched event in human
history, unrepeatable, the physical original of a moment our species only gets once. Not a copy of
it. The thing itself, of which there was exactly one.

The trade was made. \hi{Nine dollars won.}

Run the arithmetic honestly and it gets worse rather than better, because the saving was real and
measurable while the loss was neither. A supplies clerk can put nine dollars in a column. Nobody
could put a number next to \emph{the only high-quality record of the first time a member of our
species stood on another world}, so the field stayed empty, and an empty field loses every argument
with a filled one. \hi{The tape was not undervalued. It was unvalued --- and in an accounting system,
unvalued and worthless are stored in the same place.} That is the mechanism, and it has nothing to do
with secrecy. A conspiracy at least implies somebody knew what they had.

And the exchange rate got settled in public, which is the part that should sting. In 1976 a NASA
intern named Gary George bought roughly 1{,}150 surplus reels at a government auction for \$217.77 ---
about nineteen cents a tape --- intending to resell them to television stations for the tape stock. He
kept a few because the labels mentioned Apollo~11. On 20~July 2019, three of those reels sold at
Sotheby's for \$1.82~million. \hi{The same object, unchanged, priced at nineteen cents by the
institution that made it and at six hundred thousand dollars apiece by everyone else.} The people
doing the erasing were not corrupt and were not conspiring. They were following a supplies policy that
had no field for irreplaceable.

The consequence is permanent. When NASA restored the moonwalk for the fortieth anniversary, Lowry
Digital had nothing to work from but degraded NTSC copies --- broadcast dubs of a signal that had
already been downconverted before the world ever saw it. The best pictures ever taken of
the Apollo~11 EVA existed for about eleven years, were watched by nobody outside three tracking
stations, and then became blank stock. And notice the timing: the erasure happened in the early
1980s, and the public did not learn of it until August 2006. Roughly a quarter of a century passed in
which the tapes were gone and everyone assumed they were safe.

So the question stands, and it is not really about NASA. \hi{What is the correct price of a thing
that can never be made again?} Every archive, laboratory, museum, hard drive and family attic answers
it daily, usually by not asking. The Apollo tapes are the cleanest case study available because both
numbers are known: nine dollars on one side, and on the other a value so obviously enormous that no
system in the chain was able to write it down. Hold that thought, because the rest of this section is
the same failure running in the opposite direction --- people assigning enormous value to things that
are worth nothing at all.

A short catalogue of things people have found in NASA imagery, and what they actually are, because
the pattern is the lesson.

\textbf{The Face on Mars.} Viking 1 photographed a mesa in Cydonia in 1976 at about 50 metres per
pixel, with low sun angle and a data dropout forming a nostril. Richard Hoagland built a career on
it. Mars Global Surveyor imaged it at 1.5 metres per pixel in 1998 and 2001, and Mars Express in
3D: it is an eroded mesa, and the resemblance vanishes entirely.

\textbf{The Mars ``rat'', ``spoon'', ``lizard'', ``Bigfoot'', ``crab'', ``coffin'', ``doorway''} ---
all rocks, all pareidolia, several of them centimetres across. The Curiosity ``doorway'' is a
fracture gap about 30 cm high.

\textbf{Lunar ``towers'', ``obelisks'' and ``the Shard''} in Lunar Orbiter frames are film artefacts,
emulsion defects and long shadows from small rocks near the terminator.

\textbf{The ``alien on Mars'' and blue-light objects} in rover images are compression artefacts,
dust on the optics, and cosmic ray hits on the CCD --- which produce bright single-pixel streaks
that appear in one frame of a stereo pair and not the other, which is exactly how you identify
them.

\textbf{ISS live-feed cutoffs} are loss of signal during satellite handover, and the ISS feed cuts
out constantly for reasons documented in the operations schedule.

\textbf{Nibiru and Planet X} arrival dates have now failed continuously since 2003.

The underlying issue is that we now have petabytes of planetary imagery, publicly released --- and
NASA's open data policy is the reason all of this is possible, which is worth remembering when
someone tells you NASA hides things. Search a large enough noisy dataset for any shape, and you
will find it. Human visual processing is aggressively tuned for faces and animals in
low-information scenes, because a false positive on a predator costs nothing and a false negative
costs everything. \hi{Every one of these ``discoveries'' is that system working exactly as evolved.}

Put the two halves of this section side by side and you have the whole problem in one frame. An
agency threw away the irreplaceable to save the price of a blank tape, and a community spends decades
zooming into rock shadows that cost nothing to look at and mean nothing when you do. Both are
valuation errors, and both come from the same place --- nobody stopping to ask what the thing in
front of them is actually worth. \hi{The Apollo tapes were priceless and got treated as stock; a
30-centimetre fracture in a Martian outcrop is stock and gets treated as priceless.} A field that
cannot tell those apart will never find anything, and an agency that cannot tell them apart will not
be able to prove what it already found.

\begin{funfact}
The genuinely funny part is that NASA imagery \emph{does} contain a real, verified alien
discovery, and nobody who hunts for faces on Mars ever mentions it. Rosetta found glycine on comet
67P. Stardust returned samples with amino acids. Cassini flew through plumes of liquid water,
molecular hydrogen, silica and organics erupting from Enceladus, indicating hydrothermal activity
in a subsurface ocean. Titan has methane rivers and complex organic chemistry. Perseverance has
found organic molecules in Jezero crater. The most extraordinary things in the archive are in the
spectra, not the shadows --- and reading spectra requires learning something, which is the whole
reason this book exists.
\end{funfact}

\section{Testimonials from Astronauts}\label{sec:testimonialsastronauts}

Astronaut testimony is the strongest-sounding evidence in ufology and one of the weakest on
inspection, and the reason is worth understanding, because it is not that astronauts lie.

Start with the strongest card in the deck, because everything else in this section is a variation
on it. \hi{\mbox{\textbf{Gordon Cooper}} is the best witness ufology has ever had, and he is still not
good enough.} That is not a slight. Cooper was one of the original Mercury Seven, a fighter pilot
in West Germany at twenty-three, an Edwards test pilot with two thousand hours in
century-series jets, the man who slept in the capsule on the pad, flew twenty-two orbits alone in
\emph{Faith~7} and brought her home on manual control when the automatic system failed. If
trained observation under pressure counted as proof, he would have settled the question by
himself. He believed, said so publicly for forty years, and said it to the United Nations. And
when you separate what he personally saw from what he was told and from what he inferred, the
residue is thinner than the citation implies --- for reasons that have nothing to do with his
honesty and everything to do with what any single observer, however good, can actually establish.
His full case gets its own page overleaf.

\figwrap[r]{0.42}{An SLA panel drifting after separation --- the object Aldrin has repeatedly identified as what Apollo~11 saw.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apollo_SLA_panel_jettison.jpg}{fig:slapanel}
The others are weaker versions of the same shape. \textbf{Edgar Mitchell}, sixth man
on the Moon, was an unambiguous believer in a Roswell cover-up; he was also explicit that he had
seen nothing himself and was repeating what old-timers told him after the fact. \textbf{Buzz
Aldrin} described an object shadowing Apollo 11 outward, and has said repeatedly and with visible
irritation that it was one of the four SLA panels jettisoned from the S-IVB (see Figure~\ref{fig:slapanel}), which is exactly what
the trajectory requires. \textbf{Story Musgrave} has spoken about seeing a snake-like object near
the shuttle, and has said in the same breath that it was debris. \textbf{James McDivitt} saw a
cylinder on Gemini 4, said so, and spent years correcting people who inflated it. \textbf{Brian
O'Leary} and \textbf{Ed White}'s reported remarks come to us third-hand. And the famous ``Santa
Claus'' transmission from Apollo 8 was a coded announcement that Lovell's family should expect a
Christmas present, which the crew found funny at the time.

Now the pattern. \hi{Almost every case divides into two kinds: an astronaut describing an object
they themselves identified, or an astronaut expressing a private belief about events they did not
witness.} Neither is what the citation implies. The quotations circulate stripped of the sentence
that follows them, which is usually the explanation.

There is also a structural reason astronauts are unreliable narrators of orbital anomalies, and it
has nothing to do with their competence. Low Earth orbit is full of objects. Ice crystals vent from
the vehicle constantly and hang alongside it at matched velocity. Paint flecks, insulation, bolts,
and the vehicle's own jettisoned hardware travel in nearby orbits, meaning relative motion looks
slow, close and controlled when the object is small and centimetres-to-metres away rather than
large and distant. There is no atmosphere to give scale, no horizon for reference, and no
familiar-sized object in frame. A trained observer in that environment cannot judge range, size or
speed, and the good ones say so.

\hi{The argument from authority fails here in a specific and instructive way.} Being an expert in
operating a spacecraft confers no expertise in identifying unfamiliar objects outside it. That is a
different skill --- optics, photogrammetry, orbital debris analysis --- and it is the skill the
people who examine the footage afterwards actually have. Test pilots are excellent at flying and
demonstrably ordinary at estimating the distance of unidentified lights, which is the same
conclusion Section~\ref{sec:pilots} reached about pilots in the atmosphere.

None of this makes them fools. Several of these men flew machines with less computing power than a
washing machine to another world, and Mitchell in particular was a serious, curious person who
thought hard about consciousness for the rest of his life. Believing something on insufficient
evidence is not a character flaw; it is the default human condition, and the whole point of method
is that it protects people who are otherwise formidable.

\begin{funfact}
The most persistent quotation in this entire genre --- Neil Armstrong reporting that ``they''
were on the Moon and warning us off --- was invented in 1979 by a Chinese-language tabloid,
translated back, and attributed to a fictional NASA source. Armstrong, who gave almost no
interviews, publicly denied it in one of the few he did give. It remains in circulation because
nobody who repeats it wanted to check, and because a man who avoids the press is a very convenient
witness.
\end{funfact}

Because this section gets quoted at me more than any other, here is the whole population, one page
each --- Cooper first, as the strongest case and the one that deserves the most care, then the
twelve. \hi{Twelve men have walked on the surface of another world, and every one of them has been
conscripted at some point into an argument he did not make.} They are \textbf{Neil Armstrong} and
\textbf{Buzz Aldrin} (Apollo~11), \textbf{Pete Conrad} and \textbf{Alan Bean} (Apollo~12),
\textbf{Alan Shepard} and \textbf{Edgar Mitchell} (Apollo~14), \textbf{David Scott} and
\textbf{James Irwin} (Apollo~15), \textbf{John Young} and \textbf{Charles Duke} (Apollo~16), and
\textbf{Eugene Cernan} and \textbf{Harrison Schmitt} (Apollo~17). Four are still living as this is
written: Aldrin, Scott, Duke and Schmitt. What follows is what each man actually said on the
subject, which in most cases is either nothing at all or the opposite of what the internet reports.

\astroplate{L. Gordon ``Gordo'' Cooper Jr.}{Mercury Seven \textbullet{} \emph{Faith~7}, 1963 \textbullet{} Gemini~5, 1965 \textbullet{} Col.\ USAF}{mw_cooper.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (1962 official portrait). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gordon_Cooper_1962_portrait.jpg}{%
Cooper is the case to beat. Born in Shawnee, Oklahoma in 1927, he flew F-84s and F-86s with the
86th Fighter-Bomber Wing in West Germany, came home to Edwards as a test pilot on the F-102A and
F-106B, was picked for the Mercury Seven in 1959, and in May 1963 flew twenty-two orbits over
thirty-four hours in \emph{Faith~7} --- the last American to go to orbit alone --- taking manual
control for re-entry when the automatic system quit and splashing down close enough to the
carrier to be photographed from her deck. He flew again on Gemini~5 with Pete Conrad, backed up
Apollo~10, and retired a colonel in 1970.

\hi{He made two claims, and they are not the same kind of claim.} The first: that in 1951, flying
over West Germany, his squadron repeatedly saw metallic disc-shaped objects at altitudes their
aircraft could not reach. The second, and the famous one: that in 1957 at Edwards a camera crew
under his supervision filmed a saucer-like craft descending, extending three legs, settling on the
dry lake, then lifting away --- and that he sent the film up the chain to the Pentagon as ordered
and never saw it again. He told that story for the rest of his life, in a 1978 address at the
United Nations, in his memoir \emph{Leap of Faith}, and on camera shortly before his death in
2004.

Now weigh it properly, which means neither dismissing it nor rounding it up. The 1951 sightings
are a trained pilot's estimate of altitude and size for distant objects with no reference scale ---
exactly the judgement Section~\ref{sec:pilots} showed pilots to be poor at, and Cooper's own profession made
him confident rather than accurate. The 1957 film is the crux, and \hi{Cooper's position on it was
that of a supervisor, not a witness: by his account he handled the camera crew and the paperwork,
not the eyepiece.} What he could testify to first-hand was that film existed, that he forwarded it,
and that it did not come back. That is a documented-sounding claim with no document at the end of
it --- no frame of footage, no crew member on the record corroborating the landing, no film library
number. A lost film is not evidence of a saucer; it is evidence of a lost film, and the Air Force
in 1957 mislaid a great deal.

So here is the honest verdict, and it is the reason he opens this section. Cooper was not a
fantasist, not a self-promoter, and not confused about what he had seen. He was a serious man
reporting sincerely, and after sixty years his testimony has produced no object, no image and no
second witness to the central event. \hi{That is the whole lesson of this chapter in one
biography: the quality of a witness sets a ceiling on how seriously a claim should be taken, and
it never substitutes for the thing itself.} If the best witness available cannot get us past
hearsay about a missing reel, the problem is not the witnesses.}

\moonwalker{Neil A. Armstrong}{Apollo~11 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} first steps, 20 July 1969}{mw_armstrong.jpg}{Photograph: NASA. Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government). Author not individually credited.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Neil_Armstrong_pose.jpg}{%
The first man on the Moon is the most-quoted UFO witness in history and never said any of it. There
is no interview, transcript, air-to-ground loop, debriefing or memoir in which Armstrong reports a
lunar encounter. \hi{The famous warning --- that ``they'' were parked on the far rim of the crater
and that we were ordered off --- was manufactured in 1979 and attributed to an anonymous NASA
source who does not exist.} Armstrong denied it directly. His reticence is precisely why he was
chosen as the mouthpiece: a man who gave perhaps a dozen substantial interviews in forty-three
years cannot flood the zone with corrections. When he did speak about the unexplained, it was
about ice crystals, propellant venting and the difficulty of judging scale without an atmosphere
--- the mundane, correct answer.}

\moonwalker{Edwin E. ``Buzz'' Aldrin Jr.}{Apollo~11 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} second man on the Moon}{mw_aldrin.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (S69-31743). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Buzz_Aldrin_(S69-31743).jpg}{%
Aldrin is the source of the single most abused sighting in the literature, and he has spent decades
trying to take it back. On the way out to the Moon the crew watched an object holding station
alongside them. Aldrin described it honestly --- an L-shape, then a cylinder, apparently keeping
pace --- and then explained, over and over, in print, on television and to anyone who would listen,
that it was one of the four adapter panels jettisoned from the spent S-IVB stage, tumbling on the
same trajectory because it had been let go on the same trajectory. \hi{He is on record calling the
alien interpretation a misrepresentation of his own words, and has been visibly angry about it.}
That has not slowed the citation down by a single revolution, which tells you something about how
the claim is actually used.}

\moonwalker{Charles ``Pete'' Conrad Jr.}{Apollo~12 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} third man on the Moon, November 1969}{mw_conrad.jpg}{Photograph: NASA. Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Astronaut_Charles_Conrad_Jr.jpg}{%
Conrad stepped off the ladder, noted that it might have been a small step for Neil but it was a
long one for him, and proceeded to run one of the most technically productive surface missions of
the programme --- a pinpoint landing beside the derelict Surveyor~3 probe, whose sun-cooked hardware
they cut up and brought home. He reported no anomalies of any kind. He is nonetheless a fixture on
quotation lists, generally by way of remarks about the strangeness of the lunar light or the
appearance of the zodiacal glow, stripped of the sentence that identifies what he was describing.
Conrad died in 1999 following a motorcycle accident. \hi{An astronaut who never made a claim cannot
retract one, which makes the dead the most useful witnesses in ufology.}}

\moonwalker{Alan L. Bean}{Apollo~12 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} fourth man on the Moon}{mw_bean.jpg}{Photograph: NASA / Johnson Space Center (S69-38859). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alan_Bean_NASA_portrait_(S69-38859).jpg}{%
Bean is the clearest case in the set, because he was asked the question directly in 2017 and
answered it without hedging: \hi{``I do not believe that anyone from outer space has ever visited
the Earth.''} His reasoning was an engineer's. Interstellar distances are not a detail to be waved
away; a civilisation capable of crossing them would not need to hide, and the absence of any
physical trace after decades of looking is itself the evidence. Bean spent his later life as a
painter, working lunar dust from his mission patches into the pigment of canvases depicting the
surface he had walked on --- a man with an unusually strong claim to romance about the Moon, and no
patience at all for this particular story. He died in 2018.}

\moonwalker{Alan B. Shepard Jr.}{Apollo~14 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} first American in space, fifth on the Moon}{mw_shepard.jpg}{Photograph: NASA. Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government). Cropped version of a NASA original.}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alan_Shepard_Apollo_14_(cropped).jpg}{%
Shepard flew Mercury-Redstone~3 in 1961, was grounded for most of a decade by an inner-ear
disorder, fought his way back onto a flight crew and landed in the Fra Mauro highlands in 1971,
where he hit two golf balls with a contingency sample tool. He was famously unsentimental,
famously impatient, and said nothing whatsoever about extraterrestrials. \hi{Where his name appears
on sighting lists it is usually attached to a garbled version of the Mercury-era ``fireflies'' ---
which were John Glenn's, not his, and which were ice crystals shedding from the spacecraft skin.}
The misattribution survives because the two men are filed together in memory as the first
Americans up. Shepard died in 1998.}

\moonwalker{Edgar D. Mitchell}{Apollo~14 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} sixth man on the Moon}{mw_mitchell.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (1971 official portrait). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Mitchell_(1971_portrait).jpg}{%
Mitchell is the one genuine believer among the twelve, and the most instructive. He stated
repeatedly and publicly that he was satisfied a crash-retrieval programme existed and had been
concealed. He also stated, with a scrupulousness his admirers tend to omit, that \hi{he had seen
nothing himself} --- that his conviction rested on what retired officers and old-timers from the
Roswell area had told him, years after the fact, in private. That is hearsay held sincerely by a
remarkable man; it is not observation. Mitchell experienced something profound on the way home in
1971 and spent the rest of his life studying consciousness seriously. Believing a thing on
insufficient evidence is not stupidity. It is the default setting for human beings, which is
exactly why method exists. He died in 2016.}

\moonwalker{David R. Scott}{Apollo~15 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} seventh man on the Moon, July 1971}{mw_scott.jpg}{Photograph: NASA Johnson Space Center (S71-39483). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PORTRAIT_-_ASTRONAUT_DAVID_SCOTT_-_COMMANDER_(APOLLO_15)_-_CAPE_(S71-39483).jpg}{%
Scott commanded the first of the extended scientific missions, drove the first lunar rover, and
closed the final EVA by holding a geological hammer in one hand and a falcon feather in the other
and dropping them for the television camera. They hit the dust together. \hi{That is what an
astronaut looks like when he is actually testing a hypothesis in front of an audience:
falsifiable, repeatable, and over in two seconds.} Scott has never reported an unidentified object,
and when the subject arises he redirects it to what the surface work actually found --- the Genesis
Rock, the mare basalts, the ages. He is one of the four moonwalkers still living.}

\moonwalker{James B. Irwin}{Apollo~15 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} eighth man on the Moon}{mw_irwin.jpg}{Photograph: NASA Johnson Space Center (S71-39485). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PORTRAIT_-_ASTRONAUT_JAMES_B_IRWIN_-_LUNAR_MODULE_PILOT_(LMP)_(APOLLO_15)_-_CAPE_(S71-39485).jpg}{%
Irwin came home changed, left NASA within a year and founded a religious ministry, describing his
time on the surface as an encounter with God rather than with anybody else. He then mounted several
expeditions to Mount Ararat in search of the remains of Noah's Ark, and did not find them. \hi{This
is the useful case, because Irwin is proof that a moonwalker can hold an extraordinary conviction
and still produce no evidence for it.} His sincerity was total; his sample return was empty. Nobody
cites Irwin in support of the Ark, because the people who cite astronauts are not actually
interested in what astronauts believe --- only in whether the belief is the one they already hold.
Irwin died in 1991, the first of the twelve to go.}

\moonwalker{John W. Young}{Apollo~16 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} ninth man on the Moon, April 1972}{mw_young.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (1972). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Astronaut_John_W._Young_(1972).jpg}{%
Young flew Gemini, flew Apollo twice, walked in the Descartes highlands, and then commanded the
first flight of the Space Shuttle in 1981 --- six missions across three programmes, more time in
more vehicles than almost anyone. If sustained exposure to spaceflight produced encounters, Young
was the maximum-likelihood candidate, and he reported none. What he did spend his later decades
doing was writing internal memoranda warning that the agency was underestimating its own risks, on
asteroid impacts, on supervolcanoes, on the Shuttle itself. \hi{Young was the opposite of a
credulous man: he was a professional worrier who insisted on quantifying the thing he was worried
about.} He died in 2018.}

\moonwalker{Charles M. Duke Jr.}{Apollo~16 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} tenth and youngest man on the Moon}{mw_duke.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (official portrait). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Official_NASA_portrait_Charles_Moss_Duke_Jr.jpg}{%
Duke was thirty-six when he landed, the youngest of the twelve, and he left a family photograph in
the dust with all four of them on the front and a note on the back. He later became an outspoken
evangelical Christian and has spent fifty years on stages talking about the flight and his faith.
He is therefore asked about aliens constantly, by audiences primed to hear yes, and he says no ---
that he saw nothing on the way out, nothing on the surface, and nothing on the way home. \hi{A man
who will testify publicly to a religious conversion is not a man withholding testimony out of
embarrassment.} He is simply reporting what was there, which was geology. Duke is still living.}

\moonwalker{Eugene A. Cernan}{Apollo~17 \textbullet{} Commander \textbullet{} last man on the Moon, December 1972}{mw_cernan.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (S71-51308). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cernan_s71-51308.jpg}{%
Cernan is the strongest card the astronaut argument holds, and it is worth playing it fairly. Asked
about unidentified objects in 1973, he said he believed some of them were real and that they were
\hi{``somebody else, some other civilisation.''} That is a moonwalker, on the record, endorsing the
hypothesis. Note carefully what it is: a statement of opinion about reports made by other people.
Cernan did not claim to have seen anything himself on Gemini~9, Apollo~10 or Apollo~17, and in the
same era he was scathing about the notion that the landings were faked. \hi{His view was that the
universe is too large for us to be alone --- which is a position about probability, not a
sighting.} Read that way it is respectable and quite common. Read as eyewitness evidence, which is
how it is always presented, it is nothing at all. Cernan died in 2017.}

\moonwalker{Harrison H. ``Jack'' Schmitt}{Apollo~17 \textbullet{} Lunar Module Pilot \textbullet{} twelfth and last man to step onto the Moon}{mw_schmitt.jpg}{Photograph: NASA (S71-52260). Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government).}{https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Harrison_H._Schmitt.jpg}{%
Schmitt is the only professional scientist to have worked on the lunar surface --- a field geologist
with a Harvard doctorate, dropped into the Taurus-Littrow valley, where he found the orange soil at
Shorty crater and correctly read it as volcanic glass rather than the rust of an oxidising process.
He later served a term in the United States Senate and has argued for decades that the lunar
regolith's helium-3 is a strategic resource. \hi{Asked directly whether he believes aliens exist,
he answers as a scientist should: the statistical odds of life somewhere else, probably
carbon-based like ours, are very good --- and the odds of it having come here are a different
question with no evidence behind it.} That distinction, between life elsewhere and visitors here,
is the whole discipline compressed into one sentence, and it is fitting that the last man to leave
is the one who states it most cleanly. Schmitt is still living.}
