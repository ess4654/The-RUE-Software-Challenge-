Chapter-level source notes appear throughout the text. What follows is the consolidated list, plus
recommendations for going further. I have deliberately included work I disagree with, because you
cannot evaluate an argument you have only encountered in summary --- least of all in a summary written
by someone with a position.

\section*{New Media}

The centre of gravity in popular paranormal investigation has moved off television and onto
YouTube, and any honest reference list has to say so. What follows is not endorsement of the
conclusions; it is where the fieldwork actually happens now, and it should be watched the way one
watches any primary source --- attentively, and with the protocol questions from
Chapter~\ref{ch:abduction} in hand.

\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{1.2em}\setlength{\itemindent}{-1.2em}
\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}}
\item Reagan, R.\ \emph{Address to the 42nd Session of the United Nations General Assembly}, New
York, 21 September 1987 --- video of the ``alien threat from outside this world'' passage, YouTube.
The primary source for the opening of Section~\ref{sec:firstcontactdebate}. Watch the delivery
rather than reading the transcript: the line is placed deliberately, in a speech otherwise about
arms control, and the chamber's reaction is part of the evidence.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bfS7FrN5aNQ}
\item \emph{President Reagan on Aliens and Peace} --- C-SPAN video clip of the same passage, with
the surrounding remarks intact. Cited alongside the YouTube copy because C-SPAN is the archival
holder and the two together establish that the quotation is not edited.
\srclink{https://www.c-span.org/clip/white-house-event/user-clip-president-reagan-on-aliens-and-peace/4495180}
\item Sam and Colby (Sam Golbach and Colby Brock). Paranormal investigation and urban exploration
channel, YouTube, 2014--present. The most-watched paranormal investigators in history, and the
reason a generation knows what a spirit box and the Estes Method are. Unusually transparent about
nights when nothing happens, which is more than most broadcast series manage.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@SamandColby}
\item \emph{Sam and Colby}. Wikipedia --- for the biographical and audience figures cited in
Section~\ref{sec:ghostinstruments}.
\srclink{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_and_Colby}
\item Svendsen, E.\ \emph{The Science of Ufology} --- recorded lecture series, YouTube. The spoken
version of this book, delivered before it was written. Cited here as a primary source because the
arguments, case selections and running order of several chapters were worked out first in these
talks, and the recordings are the closest thing to a laboratory notebook for the project.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b0MjYaieASQ\&list=PLest4rx12kGCr8PF3kU5KqLJhi2cwtg1Z}
\item Svendsen, E.\ \emph{The Science of Ufology} --- companion lecture series, YouTube. Second
playlist; extended sessions, follow-up material and audience questions, including several of the
cases treated in Chapters~\ref{ch:aviation} and~\ref{ch:media}.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=or9Icv7vllg\&list=PLest4rx12kGAc0EDy6WH_7ha4ri94hxaO}
\item Svendsen, E.\ (\emph{mrd66}). Gallery, favourites and journals, FurAffinity. The author's own
account in the anthropomorphic-animal fandom, listed as a primary source for the fandom material
rather than as decoration. The argument of the closing pages of \emph{About the Author} --- that
hybrid imagery is mass-produced, at scale, by people who know perfectly well that no such animal
exists, and that the dragon is drawn more often than almost any real species despite having no stock
animal behind it at all --- is drawn from two decades inside this community, and a reader who
wants to check that claim should not have to take it on trust. FurAffinity is the fandom's largest
and oldest general archive, and a gallery and favourites list is the closest thing it has to a
laboratory notebook: it is dated, public, itemised, and shows exactly which parts of which real
animals were taken to assemble creatures that were never alive. For the published species figures
behind the dragon claim, see Furscience (Plante, Reysen, Roberts and Gerbasi), \emph{Species
Popularity}, from the 2020 international online survey: wolf 20.7~per~cent, dragon 16.0~per~cent,
fox 15.8~per~cent --- that is, one non-existent animal placed second among roughly forty species
categories, ahead of the fox and every other real creature but the wolf.
\srclink{https://furscience.com/research-findings/fursonas/3-1-species-popularity/}
Cited alongside
Section~\ref{sec:animalheads}, Section~\ref{sec:animalpeople} and the
\textsc{Alpha Draconians} entry in Section~\ref{sec:racesaz}. The site is age-restricted and
hosts adult material; it is referenced here for the iconography and nothing else.
\srclink{https://www.furaffinity.net/user/mrd66}
\item Project Gravitaur, YouTube. A multi-platform reconstruction project built around S-4 and
Bob Lazar's account of his work there, directed by Luigi Vendittelli in collaboration with Lazar
himself; the first release is the film \emph{S4: The Bob Lazar Story}, with a VR walkthrough of
the facility in development. Useful alongside Chapter~\ref{ch:historyarea51} for one specific
reason: it is an attempt to render a testimony spatially --- room by room, bay by bay --- which
exposes how much of any base narrative depends on a floor plan nobody outside can check. Treat the
reconstruction as an illustration of the claim, not as evidence for it.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@ProjectGravitaurOfficial}
\item \emph{Marble Hornets}, YouTube, 2009--2014. Troy Wagner and Joseph DeLage. The primary source
for the Slenderman legend as it actually spread --- eighty-seven found-footage entries plus the
adversarial \emph{totheark} channel, all of it presented as recovered tapes and none of it
explained. Cited in Section~\ref{sec:cryptozoology} as the one case in this book where a sighting
mythology can be traced to a named author and a dated post, and watched here for its technique:
degraded footage, an off-centre figure, an incomplete archive and an unreliable narrator are the
deliberate construction of exactly the evidence profile ufology treats as its strongest.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@MarbleHornets}
\item \emph{ThePolishedKnob}, YouTube, c.\,2010--present. Roughly 219 uploads, about 28{,}000
subscribers, and the primary source for the ``Aliens on YouTube'' case study in
Section~\ref{sec:influencehollywood}: a run of handheld night clips in which a tall, thin, pale
humanoid figure appears at the edge of the light, one of which has passed 900{,}000 views and has been
recirculated across TikTok and Instagram, usually stripped of any attribution. Listed as primary
source material rather than as evidence. The point of citing it is that fifteen years after upload
there is still no published frame analysis, no lighting reconstruction, no site identification and no
on-record interview with the uploader --- believers list it as genuine and sceptics dismiss it as a
hoax with equally little work done either way. Watch it with the protocol questions from
Chapter~\ref{ch:abduction} in hand, and note that the figure's morphology matches the screen tradition
described in Chapter~\ref{ch:media} before it matches anything else.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@ThePolishedKnob}
\item \emph{Spaced Out Radio} (Dave Scott), British Columbia, 2014--present. A nightly radio talk show
broadcasting live weeknights at 9\,p.m.\ Pacific from spacedoutradio.com, covering time travel,
Bigfoot, aliens and alien abduction. Scott is a former radio journalist (BCIT, 1998) with nearly
a decade in broadcast before leaving mainstream radio to found SOR in November 2014. He is also an
experiencer himself --- multiple paranormal events beginning in December 2011, including a Bigfoot
sighting at close range, recurring UFO appearances, personal contact with what he calls the Angel
of Death, and sightings of five extraterrestrial species --- which gives him a perspective on the
field that no academic sceptic and no armchair believer can match from opposite sides of the same
couch. His argument is the straightforward one: if experiencers are the people who actually live
the phenomena, why are they the last people allowed to speak before Congress? No valid or
justifiable reason has been offered. The objections the author has heard all reduce to fear of
what the testimony would mean --- fear over knowledge, humanity's oldest trope. As the final
words of Spielberg's film state: listen. The author finds SOR the most entertaining nightly
radio show since Art Bell died.
\srclink{https://spacedoutradio.com/}
\item \emph{totheark}, YouTube, 2009--2014. The companion channel to \emph{Marble Hornets}, kept
separate on purpose so that the audience had to decide which uploader to trust --- a design choice
worth noting, because it manufactures the same competing-testimony problem that real case files
have.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@totheark}
\item \emph{Spirit Science} (Jordan Duchnycz), YouTube, 2011--present. Animated esoteric series, and
one of the largest single channels shaping how a lay audience frames Atlantis, sacred geometry and
consciousness. Cited in Chapter~\ref{ch:atlantis} for its central claim --- that Atlantis and
dynastic Egypt are one continuous transmission, with Thoth as the ``bridge node'' who carries the
knowledge out of the drowned civilisation and into the Nile valley, thereby explaining the
pyramids, the hieroglyphic corpus and the mystery schools in a single move. There is no
archaeological support for the link, and the Egyptian material is read through a nineteenth-century
Theosophical lens rather than through the sources. It is listed because it is where a very large
number of readers meet the idea first, and because the ``bridge node'' manoeuvre --- one figure who
conveniently spans the gap in the record --- is the same move this book flags throughout the
ancient-astronaut literature.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/@SpiritScienceOfficial}
\item \emph{The Joe Rogan Experience}, episode~\#1191 --- Peter Boghossian and James Lindsay (Joe
Rogan, 4~October 2018). The long-form account of the grievance studies affair by two of the three
authors: how the twenty papers were written, which journals accepted them, and what the acceptances
show about what peer review is actually screening for. This is the interview behind
Section~\ref{sec:deconstructingpeerreview}, and it is listed as a primary source because the authors
describe their own method in their own words.
\srclink{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZZNvT1vaJg}
\item Boghossian, P., Lindsay, J.\ \& Pluckrose, H.\ Academic Grievance Studies and the Corruption
of Scholarship --- the authors' own methods write-up and full paper-by-paper results, originally
published in \emph{Areo Magazine} (2~October 2018) and archived at New Discourses. The place to
check the claims in Section~\ref{sec:deconstructingpeerreview} against the record: the count of
submissions and acceptances, the journals involved, the reviewer comments, and the authors'
statement of what the project does and does not demonstrate.
\srclink{https://newdiscourses.com/2018/10/academic-grievance-studies-corruption-scholarship/}
\item New Discourses (James Lindsay), \srclink{https://newdiscourses.com} --- the authors' ongoing
writing and podcast archive, including the grievance studies documentation and the fabricated papers
themselves.
\end{list}
